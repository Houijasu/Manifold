# M5-F2 — UCI toggle smoke sweep

> ## What this document is, and what it is NOT
>
> This is a **functional smoke test of the UCI option surface**. Every `type check`
> toggle the engine advertises was flipped away from its shipped default and made to
> play 10 games against the same binary at defaults. The question it answers is:
>
> **"Does any toggle, when flipped, break the engine — crash, forfeit on time, or play
> an illegal move?"**
>
> Answer: **no. 30 of 30 toggles passed.**
>
> **This document ranks nothing.** Ten games carries roughly **±200 Elo** of error, which
> is an order of magnitude wider than any of the effects these toggles actually have. The
> Elo column below is reproduced only because fastchess prints it; **every number in it is
> noise and none of it is evidence.** A toggle showing "+147 Elo" here and a toggle showing
> "-147 Elo" here are, at n=10, indistinguishable from each other and from zero. Nothing in
> this file may be cited as a reason to change a default.
>
> **The real per-toggle measurements are the 300-game matches in `MSN-S1` … `MSN-S7`**,
> indexed in §5. Those are the documents to cite. This one exists to prove the option
> surface is not booby-trapped.

## 1. Purpose and provenance

The engine advertises 30 `Use*` selectivity toggles. They are driven by the ablation
anchors in `crates/mf-uci/tests/bench_cli.rs`, by `harness/isolate_options.py`, and
(from M5-F4 onward) by the SPSA tuner, which sets options per game via `setoption`. A
toggle that is advertised but broken — one that panics the engine, or hangs it past its
clock — would be discovered by the tuner as a mysterious mid-session failure. This sweep
retires that risk up front.

**Both arms are the same executable.** The only difference between arm A (`flipped`) and
arm B (`default`) is one `setoption` value. This is the strictest possible single-variable
control — it removes compilation, linking, and net-loading as confounders entirely.

The seed being shared across all 30 matches means every toggle faced the **same 5 opening
pairs**. That makes the runs comparable to each other as *functional* exercises (each
toggle was asked the same questions), and it is deliberately not a sampling strategy that
would support a strength claim.

### Enumeration

Toggles were read from the **live handshake** of the binary under test, not from source:

```
py experiments/MSN-M5-sweep/probe_toggles.py target/release/manifold.exe
```

The full captured handshake is `uci-handshake.txt`. It advertises 31 `type check`
options. Thirty are swept. **`UCI_Chess960` is deliberately excluded**: it is a *rules*
toggle, not a search toggle. Flipping it makes the engine interpret castling rights under
Chess960 conventions while the opening book still serves standard positions, so the two
arms would be playing different games rather than searching the same game differently —
a broken match, not a failed toggle. Per the feature spec, `Hash`, `Threads`, `EvalFile`
and `Move Overhead` are also out of scope (they are not `type check`).

## 2. Pass criteria

Per toggle, all three must hold:

1. `run_match.ps1` exits **0**. The driver exits 3 on any inadmissible run.
2. **Zero** time forfeits, crashes, and illegal moves played, **on both arms** — cross-checked
   by the harness against both the PGN (authoritative, per-game) and the fastchess console
   summary.
3. All 10 games complete.

Any failure was defined in advance as a **real defect**: the sweep stops and escalates.
It never had to.

### Reading the Elo column (please don't)

`UseCaptureLMR=false` shows +147.19 and `UseContHistory=false` shows -147.19. Both are
5-1-4 and 1-5-4 records — a **four-game swing out of ten**. At this sample size a ±200 Elo
interval is the honest read, and the printed `± 74.15` is itself unreliable because
fastchess's interval narrows when the pentanomial happens to be concentrated, which at
n=5 pairs is luck rather than information.

The clearest demonstration that the column is noise: **`UseCaptureLMR=false` scores
positively here (+147), while the 300-game `MSN-S7` match measured turning capture LMR
*on* at +11.59 ± 22.22 Elo** — i.e. this sweep's point estimate has the wrong sign against
a real measurement of the same toggle, and is off by an order of magnitude in size. That
is exactly the failure mode this header warns about, observed in-sample.

## 4. Was the sweep vacuous? (flag-liveness control)

A sweep in which `setoption` silently ignored every name would also produce 30 clean
passes. That is the one way this result could be worthless, so it was tested directly with
`harness/isolate_options.py` (fixed depth 12, position `midgame`, same binary, stdin held
open). Full output in `flag-liveness-control.txt`:

| arm | nodes | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| defaults | 45,279 | 1.00x |
| `UseNMP=false` | 78,660 | 1.74x |
| `UseLMR=false` | 276,677 | 6.11x |
| `UseQSearchChecks=true` | 48,781 | 1.08x |

The toggles demonstrably reach the search: disabling LMR inflates the tree 6.1x at the
same depth. The options are live, so the 30 clean passes mean the engine survived 30 real
behavioural changes.

## 6. Run-condition caveat (recorded, does not affect the verdict)

`run-metadata.txt` records pre-run CPU per match. Because the 30 matches ran
back-to-back, several started while the previous match's 16 engine processes were still
tearing down, so recorded pre-run load exceeded the mission's 20% idle gate on 9 runs
(peak: `UseHistoryPruning` at 71%, `UseCorrHistPawn` at 44%, `UseCorrHistMajor` at 34%).

This is recorded rather than corrected because it does not threaten the claim being made:

- The gate exists to protect **timing** measurements. **No timing or Elo claim is made
  here.**
- CPU contention makes time forfeits **more** likely, not less. Passing the forfeit
  criterion under elevated load is a *stronger* functional result than passing it idle. The
  sweep's one real finding — zero forfeits in 300 games — survives the caveat with room to
  spare.

Had any toggle failed, this caveat would have mattered enormously (an inadmissible run
must not be read as a defect), and the correct response would have been to rerun that
toggle on a verified-idle machine before escalating. Nothing failed, so no rerun was owed.

## 7. Verdict

**PASS — the UCI option surface is functionally sound.** All 30 advertised `type check`
toggles can be flipped from their shipped defaults without crashing the engine, forfeiting
on time, or producing an illegal move, across 300 games and 30 real behavioural
configurations. **No default is changed on the strength of this sweep, and none should
be.** The SPSA work in M5-F4/F5 can drive these options via `setoption` without a
functional caveat.
