## Lead Notes

**Findings 2, 3, and 13 are one architectural decision, not three fixes.** The search is fully synchronous — `handle_go` blocks the reader loop until `search` returns — so `stop`, `ponderhit`, streamed `info`, and `go infinite` are all unreachable without moving the search off the input thread (or polling stdin from inside `SearchContext::visit_node`). Decide the concurrency model first; findings 3 and 13 largely fall out of it. Finding 2 is independent of that choice but shares the `mf-uci` position-handling code.

**Findings 1, 5, and 18 are also one cluster** — all three trace to `from_fen` accepting illegal positions. Adding validation fixes the crash and removes the justification for the search workaround, but it will reject `search_invariants.rs`'s mate corpus entry at line 13 and possibly others, so the corpus needs auditing in the same change.

**On finding 5 specifically:** this is the one finding where production behavior was shaped to satisfy a test rather than the reverse. The comment is honest about it, which is good, but the engine now knowingly declines mate in 1 in a class of positions. Worth resolving before the corpus grows.

**Not findings, for the record:** `clippy --all-targets -D warnings` is clean. The TT's XOR-verification lockless scheme (`transposition_table.rs:89-116`) is correctly ordered and the const-assert block pinning cluster size to a cache line is good practice. `Move`/`MoveFlag` bit packing is correct, including the `& 0b1011` promotion decode across capture and non-capture flags, and the all-zero-move reservation the TT relies on genuinely cannot collide with a generated move. Zobrist incremental updates are backed by a full recompute-and-compare differential test. The SEE greedy algorithm is validated against an exhaustive oracle over ~40k random reachable positions — the complexity flagged in finding 7 is a cost problem, not a correctness one.
